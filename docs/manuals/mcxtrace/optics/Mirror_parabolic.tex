\section{The \texttt{Mirror\_parabolic} McXtrace Component}
Idealized parabolic mirror (in XZ)

\subsection*{Identification}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Author:} Erik Knudsen
  \item \textbf{Origin:} Risoe
  \item \textbf{Date:} Feb 11, 2010
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Description}
Takes a reflectivity as input and reflects rays in a ideal geometry parabolic mirror. The mirror is positioned in the zx-plane curving towards positive y. The axis of the paraboloid is y, so the focal point lies at (0, f\_x, 0) with f\_x = a\textasciicircum{}2/4 for the x-curve, and f\_z = b\textasciicircum{}2/4 for the z-curve. For a != b the mirror is astigmatic: there are two different focal lengths, not a single focus.

Example: Mirror\_parabolic(R0=1, a=1, b=0, xwidth=0.02, yheight=0, zdepth=0.05)

\subsection*{Input parameters}
Parameters in \textbf{boldface} are required; the others are optional.

\begin{longtable}{p{0.22\textwidth}p{0.12\textwidth}p{0.46\textwidth}p{0.14\textwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Name} & \textbf{Unit} & \textbf{Description} & \textbf{Default} \\
\midrule
\endhead
R0 & 1 & Reflectivity of mirror. & 1 \\
a & sqrt(m) & Transverse curvature scale, if zero - the mirror is flat along x. & 1 \\
b & sqrt(m) & Longitudinal curvature scale, if zero, flat along z. & 1 \\
xwidth & m & Width of the mirror, measured as arc length s along the parabola from the vertex (not the projected extent). & 0.1 \\
zdepth & m & Length of the mirror, measured as arc length s along the parabola from the vertex (not the projected extent). & 0.1 \\
yheight & m & Thickness of mirror, one-sided: tolerance on the substrate side (below the surface). If 0 (the default) the mirror is mathematically thin. Only has an effect for rays grazing the mirror edge-on. & 0 \\
focusx & m & Transverse focal length along X. Sets a. & 0 \\
focusz & m & Longitudinal focal length along Z. Sets b. & 0 \\
radius & m & Focal length (historical name); sets focusx and focusz together. & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}
